EDgames · design proposal · round two

Three more ways the site could stop looking like a first draft

The brief was that something in the current design feels amateur. Below is what I think that something is, and then three complete directions built on the real catalog — same nineteen games, same Hebrew, same age ranges. Every one is a working page you can click around in, on a phone or on a laptop.

These are not the three you saw before

The first round offered Playground, Library and Arcade, and you turned all three down. Those are gone and nothing here is a reshuffle of them. The first round mostly varied the surface — flat, editorial, dark. This round varies the answer: one direction fixes the identity, one fixes the shape of the page, and one fixes the device it is actually used on. They cannot all be right, which is the point.

One thing did survive, because it was the accurate part and it is still true: the single biggest tell is the artwork. What is new is a way to fix it that does not cost nineteen drawings — see the first item below.

What reads as amateur

Not a taste problem. Five specific things, and the first does most of the damage.

1

The picture on every card is an emoji

A 62px emoji floating in a pastel square is exactly what a placeholder looks like, and the glyph is the machine's drawing, not yours — it is a different picture on a phone, a Mac and the Windows box the site is played on. All three directions below replace it with one drawn mark per subject: seven drawings, not nineteen, with composition and field weight doing the rest so no two games in a subject wear the same cover. A game added next month inherits a mark for free.

2

Frosted glass over drifting blurred blobs

That was the house look in 2021. On a near-white mint canvas the glass does not read as glass — it softens every edge and leaves the page looking slightly out of focus. None of the three directions uses it.

3

The pastels are too weak to do a job

The subject tints cannot carry text, which is why the stylesheet needs a second "deepened ink" for every one of them. Colour that has to be rescued by a fallback is not working. The palette here is seven saturated colours that each clear the contrast floor against white on their own.

4

Nineteen identical cards in one flat grid

No featured game, no sections, nothing with more weight than anything else. A parent opening it has no idea where to start, so the page reads as a list rather than as a place. Directions 2 and 3 each answer this, and answer it differently.

5

It is laid out for a screen it is not used on

A 1180px grid reflowed into two narrow columns is what an unfinished site looks like on a tablet — which is the thing a five-year-old actually plays this on, and the thing you are reading this on right now.

Three directions

Each is a real page rendering the real catalog, so what you see is the actual set of games and the actual Hebrew, not filler. Click a thumbnail to open it full size — and open at least one of them on a phone, because two of the three are designed for that first.

Direction 1

Poster כרזה

Commit to everything. The site does not look amateur because it is ugly — it looks amateur because nothing on it is committed to. So: one near-black ink instead of soft greys, seven saturated subject colours strong enough to be a field with white on top, a display face at four times the body size, a hard rule under the header instead of a floating glass bar, and the drawn marks instead of emoji. Same page, same grid, same filters. Everything is simply turned up until it means something.

Type
Secular One display + Heebo body
Fixes
Tells 1, 2, 3 — and 4 partly, with a featured strip
Cost
Lowest. A stylesheet swap plus the seven marks. The grid, the filters and the markup stay as they are.
Risk
Lowest, and the least ambitious. It is still a grid of cards — a better one, but a grid.

Direction 2

Trail מסלול

Stop being a list, start being a place. The grid is gone. There is one trail, walked from the top, with three stations along it, and the games are stops on the way — each on its own side of the road, with the subject's mark on the marker. Scrolling is the browsing. A child can be shown where they are without reading anything, and the page finally has a beginning, a middle and an end.

The honest cost: a trail gives every game exactly one home. Your filter deliberately does the opposite — a game marked 3–5 appears under both 3–4 and 5–6, because overlap is right for a filter. It is wrong for a path, where the same game turning up twice reads as a bug. So the stations here key off the age a game starts at, and each game is placed once. That is a real change to how the catalog is organised, not just to how it is painted.

Type
Fredoka display + Heebo body
Fixes
All five, and 4 more completely than anything else here
Cost
Highest. The grid, the filter rows and the URL state all go or change, and "one home per game" is a decision, not a stylesheet.
Risk
A trail with nineteen stops is long, and it gets longer with every game you add. Past about thirty it wants sub-trails.

Direction 3

App אפליקציה

Design it for the device it is used on. A real app shell: a short top bar, one rail of subjects, content in rows rather than a wall, and a fixed bottom bar so the two things a grown-up does — change age, search — are always under a thumb. A "carry on from yesterday" card is the whole first screen. On a laptop the bottom bar becomes a side rail and the rows wrap; nothing is designed twice.

Rows suit this catalog specifically: nineteen games across seven subjects is exactly the shape that reads well as rails and badly as a wall. It is also the direction that most rewards the seven marks — a row is a subject, so the covers have to vary within one, and that is what the composition and field weight are for.

Type
Heebo 400–900, one family throughout
Fixes
All five, and 5 is the one only this direction really answers
Cost
Middling. The catalog and the filters survive intact; the page shell and the game page's chrome are rebuilt.
Risk
It is the most conventional of the three — competent, and it looks like every other kids' app. Judge it on the phone, not here.

If you want a recommendation

Direction 3, and take the marks from it whichever way you go. The site is played on a tablet and judged on a phone, and it is the only one of the three that is actually designed for that. It is also the cheapest of the two ambitious ones: the catalog, the age overlap rule and the filter logic all survive it untouched.

Direction 1 is the fallback if you want something visible this week — it is a stylesheet and seven small SVGs, and it fixes three of the five tells on its own. Nothing in it blocks doing 3 later; the palette and the marks are the same set.

Direction 2 is the one with the most charm and the most risk. It is here because it is worth knowing whether you want a place rather than a catalogue before you spend anything, not because I think it is the safe call. If it makes you smile, that is a real signal — say so and I will cost it properly.

What these are and are not

They are

  • Working pages. Filter chips toggle, the grid in direction 1 really filters, rails scroll, and every layout is responsive down to phone width.
  • Rendering the real catalog — they load assets/catalog.js, so the games, the Hebrew, the age ranges and the בקרוב marks are the live ones, not invented.
  • Keeping the house rules that are correctness rather than taste: logical CSS properties so RTL and LTR both lay out, LTR wrappers on the number ranges, 44px+ tap targets, visible focus, and prefers-reduced-motion honoured.
  • Checked on screen at 1280px and at 390px, in a real headless browser, not just read back off the source.

They are not

  • Hebrew and English. The 🌐 button is drawn but does nothing — the i18n layer is untouched and carries over unchanged whichever direction wins.
  • Wired to anything. Every card links to #; no game opens.
  • Covering the game page. game.html and the thirteen game modules each need a pass, and how big that pass is differs between the three.
  • Contrast-audited by a tool. The seven subject colours were each computed against white and clear 4.9:1 or better, but the full page has not been through a checker. That is a step before any of this ships.
  • A finished mark set. The seven drawings are the argument, at the fidelity that makes the argument. A real pass would tighten them.
